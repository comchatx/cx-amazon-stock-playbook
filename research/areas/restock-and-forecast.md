## Amazon's own rules

### A. The Restock Inventory report: what it is, where, how fresh

**A1. Definition, role, availability, freshness.** [official] "Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) Reports", https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-fba (page `updatedAt` 2026-10-13, read 2026-11-10). `GET_RESTOCK_INVENTORY_RECOMMENDATIONS_REPORT`: "Provides recommendations on products to restock, suggested order quantities, and reorder dates. Content updated in near real-time." Roles on that page: Pricing, Amazon Fulfillment. "Amazon store availability: All stores except India (IN)". It is a tab-delimited flat file that can only be requested, not scheduled. The same page's general note applies to it as a near real-time FBA report: "A near real-time FBA report is generated no more than once every 30 minutes. This means that after a near real-time FBA report is generated following your report request, a 30-minute waiting period must pass before Amazon will generate an updated version of that report." (verifier, re-read 2026-11-10)

**A2. Columns, as Amazon lists them** (same page). `Country`, `Product Name`, `FNSKU`, `Merchant SKU`, `ASIN`, `Condition`, `Supplier`, `Supplier part no.`, `Currency code`, `Price`, `Sales last 30 days`, `Units Sold Last 30 Days`, `Total Units`, `Inbound`, `Available`, `FC transfer`, `FC Processing`, `Customer Order`, `Unfulfillable`, `Working`, `Shipped`, `Receiving`, `Fulfilled by`, `Total Days of Supply` (the page adds "including units from open shipments"), `Days of Supply at Amazon Fulfillment Network`, `Alert`, `Recommended replenishment qty`, `Recommended ship date`, `Recommended action` (the page: available in the US store), `Unit storage size` (the page: US, CA, UK, DE, FR, IT, ES).

The developer-docs page gives the column names and nothing else: no definition of any column, no statement of which sales window or forecast drives `Recommended replenishment qty`, and no date column. Amazon's glossary for these columns sits on a Seller Central help page ("Restock Inventory report", linked from the forum post in A5 as `help/hub/reference/external/201824360`, id read from the thread's HTML by the verifier) that this seat could not read (*Open questions* Q1).

**A3. What Amazon says the recommendation is built from.** Three first-hand statements, none of them a formula:

- [official] sell.amazon.com, "Inventory management techniques and best practices", https://sell.amazon.com/learn/inventory-management (re-fetched by the verifier 2026-11-10 at that URL, HTTP 200; both quotations found): "When sellers choose to use FBA, they automatically gain access to Amazon's machine learning-based inventory-management system. This system uses inputs like the cost of goods sold (COGS), shipment time, and other Amazon data to forecast customer demand and set optimum inventory levels." On the same page, quoted from "Kevin G., FBA Inventory Optimization Team": "Amazon's inventory management tools use world-class machine learning algorithms to create customized restock strategies, taking into account current inventory levels, restock preferences, supply chain constraints, and anticipated demand."
- [official] News_Amazon, "Plan FBA inventory levels using a new metric", 2023-12-19, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/1aa7614a-5542-4529-abe1-ddf452681630: "Restock recommendations will continue to provide you with specific recommended ship-by dates and replenishment quantities for your next incoming shipments to help you maintain the Minimum Inventory Level."
- [official] listing playbook, 2026-10-14, not re-read (Inventory Performance Index help page, ids G202174810 / GZJF4DY2W6MERBAL): "The Restock today button shows the number of SKUs for which the days of supply is less than the restock lead time."

So Amazon states the inputs (a demand forecast, current stock, the seller's restock settings, lead time) and the target (stay above the Minimum Inventory Level), and publishes no method.

**A4. The settings a seller keys in, which change the recommendation.** [official] News_Amazon (UK), "Take advantage of restock recommendation in Send to Amazon", 2024-05-13, https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/42f1aae6-db61-4198-be0f-80c9408e4caa: "You can also provide SKU-level details for the following parameters to personalize the recommendations: Lead time, Minimum order quantity, Replenishment frequency, Case pack quantity". [official] News_Amazon (Australia), "New Fulfilment by Amazon restock recommendation feature in Send to Amazon", 2024-09-24, https://sellercentral.amazon.com.au/seller-forums/discussions/t/6358ccd6-2bb5-41bb-8fa4-90ad98948e7f: "This feature gives you SKU-level recommendations on quantity to send in based on the historic performance on four input factors, being, lead time, minimum order quantity, replenishment frequency and case pack quantity … You can also manually input lead time, minimum order quantity, replenishment frequency and case pack quantity information for your SKUs to help personalise the recommendations. Note: These recommendations are a guide only and shouldn't be used as a substitute for your own judgement about demand for your products."

The report has `Supplier` and `Supplier part no.` columns; it has no column for the lead time, minimum order quantity, replenishment frequency or case pack that produced the row. A reader of the file cannot tell whether a row was computed on the seller's own lead time or on Amazon's default (see *Only in Seller Central*).

**A5. Amazon says the Restock report will be phased out, and until then is the better of the two.** [official] KJ_Amazon, "Inventory Planning vs. Restock Report", 2025-02-24, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/f4dc91de-76c3-4892-bfc8-ac1f3695f3de: "Currently, the Restock Inventory report is your best source for restock recommendations, including suggested quantities and ship dates … You can tailor these recommendations by adjusting parameters like lead time and case pack quantities. Important update: The Restock Inventory report will be phased out after we add all critical metrics from this report to the FBA Inventory report." No date is given. As of 2026-11-10 the report type is on the developer-docs page with no deprecation label, and the SP-API deprecations page (`updatedAt` 2026-09-09) does not list it. The post describes the destination report as "a consolidated view of your sales (7, 30, 60, and 90 days), shipment statuses (working, received, and shipped)…", the same words developer-docs uses for `GET_FBA_INVENTORY_PLANNING_DATA` (B1), so "FBA Inventory report" in the post is that report type. The post carries one timestamp, 2025-02-24, and is marked "updated by moderator". In the replies [opinion, a seller, 2025-03-20/21]: "The FBA Inventory report doesn't include inactive listings … it also doesn't include out of stock listings"; KJ_Amazon answered that they "reached out to our partner team with your question/suggestion about a larger scope for the FBA inventory report" and did not dispute the omission. (Verifier re-read the thread 2026-11-10.)

An earlier seller thread with a near-identical title (sellercentral-europe, 2024-06-20, thread c43f712e-ce45-4b01-9c5b-07301e2b6664) [opinion, a seller]: "the IP report seems to be very outdated. Reports on June 18-20th have old data from 6-17 (including old send in dates of 6-17) … Recommendations from IP report and Restock report don't match up." The replies served to a plain GET are all from sellers; no Amazon reply is visible, and nothing ties KJ_Amazon's 2025 post to it. One reply [opinion, a seller]: "the restock report includes inbound so it will be more accurate. I have found the planning to not include inbound".

**A6. Amazon's FBM twin uses 14 days.** [official] News_Amazon, "Get personalized restock recommendations for your inventory", 2025-03-14 (updated 2025-03-19), https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/33b8ff50-5efb-456d-a2d3-12bcf9b86231: for Fulfilled by Merchant listings, "It calculates suggested restock quantities and restock-by dates for your products that meet specific criteria, including active sales in the last three months, projected demand, and current days of supply less than 14 days. Restock recommendations provide you with: Suggested restock quantities based on current inventory and projected two-week demand across all locations. Estimated days of supply based on the projected demand for your products. Forecasted eight-week demand trends using artificial intelligence models." The 2025-03-19 update reads: "currently available to approximately 50% of US-based Fulfilled by Merchant sellers as part of a phased roll out. We expect all Fulfilled by Merchant sellers to have access in the My Inventory page by April 18, 2025." The same announcement text was posted on the India forum on 2025-02-12 (https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/e39e97ab-9201-48ff-99cb-cb5deb311289, verifier read 2026-11-10). These are the only Amazon pages found (this seat's pass and a verifier web search on 2026-11-10) that use a 14-day days-of-supply line; it is an FBM feature's selection rule, not an FBA rule (see D4).

### C. Minimum Inventory Level and historical days of supply

**C1. Minimum Inventory Level (MIL).** [official] News_Amazon, 2023-12-19 (URL in A3): "We leverage advanced machine learning models to analyze demand forecasts and replenishment settings and recommend the minimum number of units per product that you should have on hand in our fulfillment centers … Sellers who maintain units above the Minimum Inventory Level see a 15% increase in sales over a four-week period on average, though actual results may vary." [official, via 领星] the field help 领星 reproduces (https://www.lingxing.com/help/article/fbaInventory): "最低库存水平是一项指标，可帮助您规划亚马逊物流库存补货…它是在下一个入库货件送达之前，我们建议您在亚马逊配送网络中维持的最低亚马逊物流可售库存商品数量。" (the minimum sellable FBA units Amazon recommends keeping in its network until the next inbound shipment arrives). [official] listing playbook, 2026-10-14, not re-read: "Minimum Inventory Level is a forward-looking metric measured in units".

MIL is in units and forward-looking; historical days of supply is in days and backward-looking; the fee in F is assessed on the second, not the first. A SKU can sit above MIL and still be under 28 historical days, or the reverse.

**C2. Health status values.** [official, via 领星] (same page): "缺货： 您的现有可售库存为零。低库存： 您的现有可售库存已降至最低库存水平以下。" plus 冗余 (excess) and 良好 (healthy): out of stock = available is zero; low stock = available has fallen below MIL. The column is `fba-inventory-level-health-status`.

**C3. Historical days of supply.** [official] Bryce_Amazon, "Low-inventory-level fee further explained", 2023-12-07, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/aa9f98e5-fa91-4949-af8f-6cf918c86a77: a short-term (30-day) and a long-term (90-day) figure; the FBA Inventory page's "Historical days of supply" displays "the greater of" the two. [official] listing playbook, 2026-10-14, not re-read: historical days of supply is "average daily inventory units on hand divided by average daily shipped units". The planning report carries all three (`historical-days-of-supply`, `Short term…`, `Long term…`).

**C4. MIL and capacity.** [official] News_Amazon 2023-12-19: "Your capacity limits will allow you to carry enough inventory to avoid the low-inventory-level fee, provided you don't utilize your capacity to hold excess inventory." A seller's counter-example [opinion, a seller, 2025-06-01, thread 1577f8ce-84dc-4251-88cd-2cc7f98cd0a6]: a SKU selling 17 units a week with 6 on hand was refused a 12-unit shipment with the message "You have high days of supply for this SKU in our fulfillment centers. Remove this SKU from the shipment. For more information, go to Managing FBA product level shipment restrictions." AmpliSell [opinion, "The Custom Forecasting System That Outperforms Amazon's Restock Tool", https://www.amplisell.com/blog-posts/the-custom-forecasting-system-that-outperforms-amazons-restock-tool, re-fetched by the verifier 2026-11-10] reports the same: "Amazon would recommend replenishing, say, 55 units. We'd send 54, only to be told to remove the SKU from the shipment because the 'days of supply were too high.'" Amazon has a product-level shipment restriction separate from the account's capacity limit; its help page ("Managing FBA product level shipment restrictions") was not read (Q3). What Amazon has said of it on the forum is in G6.

### D. Amazon's demand forecasts and days-of-supply lines

**D1. No SP-API read returns a seller's per-SKU demand forecast.** Established by reading the report-type pages, the Data Kiosk schemas and the models at `db2936aa`: the only forecast report type is `GET_VENDOR_FORECASTING_REPORT` (role BA, vendors only); `GET_FBA_SNS_FORECAST_DATA` was deprecated 2025-06-25 and removed 2025-12-11 (survey `03-job-stock.md`, not re-read at the deprecations page for this row). What a seller gets through the five roles is the forecast's outputs: `Recommended replenishment qty`, `Recommended ship date`, `fba-minimum-inventory-level`, `days-of-supply`, `estimated-excess-quantity`.

**D2. The forecast exists behind another door.** [official] Amazon's own Claude plugin 1.0.0 (Apache-2.0), local copy `scratch/sp-api-plugins/amazon-plugin-zip/skills/seller-analytics/references/metrics-catalog.md`, lists private-beta connector metrics in domain `Threep_Inventory`: `FBA_FORECAST_N7_P80`, `FBA_FORECAST_N30_P80`, `FBA_FORECAST_N60_P80` (7-, 30- and 60-day forecasts at the 80th percentile), `FBA_INV_VIS_ONHAND_PMEAN_DOS` and `…_P90_DOS`, `FBA_INV_VIS_TOTAL_PMEAN_DOS` and `…_P90_DOS`, `FBA_INV_VIS_RESTOCK_ORDER_DATE`, `FBA_INV_RESTOCK_QTY`, `FBA_INV_VIS_RECOMMENDED_MIL_PMEAN_DOS` and `…_P90_DOS`, `FBA_INV_HIL_ZONE`. They are served only by the connector at `sellingpartner-ai.amazon.com`, which is not SP-API, has no role on the Role Mappings pages, and was not called. The names show that Amazon computes days of supply against both a mean and a 90th-percentile forecast, and a recommended MIL expressed in days as well as units.

**D3. "Excess" is 90 days of forecast demand.** [official] listing playbook, 2026-10-14, not re-read (IPI help page): "We consider an item to be excess if it has over 90 days of supply based on the forecasted demand." The fee floor in F is 28 days of *historical* shipments. The two bounds are measured on different bases.

**D4. The "14 days" line in Amazon's plugin.** [official] `skills/stockout-prevention/SKILL.md` in the same plugin computes `unitCount / 30` from `getOrderMetrics` over 30 days and `fulfillableQuantity / avg_daily_units`, bands the result at 7 and 21 days, and says "Amazon's own analysis rule is *days of supply < 14 → at risk*". The only Amazon page found with a 14-day line is the FBM feature in A6. The bands are that skill's; this pack borrows none.

**D5. Data Kiosk's AWD simulation defaults.** [official] local schema `models/schemas/data-kiosk/analytics_economics_2024_03_15.graphql` (about lines 594–622), `ProductInputContext`: `historicalAverageWeeklySales` "Default value calculated based on the average weekly sales in the past 90 days"; `sendToAWDQuantity` "Default value is 12 weeks of Historical Weekly Sales"; `shipmentLeadTime` "(weeks) … Default value is 1 week"; `unitPerCarton` "Default value is 20 units per carton". These are defaults of a cost simulation, not recommendations.

### E. AWD auto-replenishment and Amazon-managed supply

**E1. Trigger.** [official] Topher_Amazon, "How AWD auto-replenishment quantities are calculated and triggered", 2024-10-31, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/3bd483ae-84cf-4c42-899d-88b4c659540a: "When the combined quantity of Available inventory in FBA network and Inventory in transit to FBA network falls below the Optimum level of supply for a specific ASIN, the Auto-Replenishment will be triggered during the next scheduled replenishment cycle … Each seller-ASIN will have its own optimal level, and this model runs 7 days a week to monitor inventory in the FBA network and determine if an Auto-Replenishment shipment needs to be triggered". In symbols: optimum level − available in FCs − in transit to FCs > 0. The "Optimum level of supply" is Amazon's, shown on the AWD inventory dashboard; no API field carries it (the AWD model at `db2936aa` has `replenishmentQuantity`, "in transit from AWD and has not yet been received at FBA", and no optimum level or auto-replenishment setting).

**E2. What Amazon says it is.** [official] sell.amazon.com, "Amazon Warehousing and Distribution (AWD)", https://sell.amazon.com/programs/warehousing (the helper's URL `/fulfillment-by-amazon/warehousing` returns 404; the canonical URL was read from the download and re-fetched by the verifier 2026-11-10, quotation found): "Auto-replenishment relies on a proprietary data-science model that monitors the inventory levels in Prime-ready fulfillment centers and triggers replenishments in order to maintain adequate inventory levels … If you leave auto-replenishment enabled, your products are considered in stock and buyable when they're received by AWD". [official] https://sell.amazon.com/supply-chain (`/programs/supply-chain` redirects there; verifier re-read 2026-11-10): "With auto-replenishment, you never have to worry about FBA capacity limits or low-inventory charges. Keep in mind that the promised delivery dates for units in AWD facilities (or in transit from AWD to a fulfillment center) will differ from delivery dates for units that are already at a fulfillment center."

**E3. Its interaction with the bounds.** [official] Indy_Amazon, 2026-08-04 (URL in H2): "AWD auto-replenished inventory is NOT subject to FBA capacity limits during peak". The low-inventory-level fee exempts SKUs replenished through AWD (2023: auto-replenished; 2027: at least 70% of inventory auto-replenished via AWD over the prior 90 days, [official, via PPC Land]). The seller gives up the quantity decision: a seller reported [opinion, 2025-02-06, thread cff7…] "AWD recently auto-replenished six months' worth of inventory for an ASIN" and asked whether FBA storage rates then apply.

**E4. Time.** [official] moderator Topher, 2024-08-27, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/41814845-f32a-4453-9c26-65dfe9409a2c: "Shipments inbound to AWD typically take 2-4 days once they arrive to be received at the AWD site. Separately, automated replenishment and manual replenishment takes an average of 14 days for the inventory to move from an AWD distribution center to the FBA network." Sellers on Amazon's forums report more than 20 days [opinion]. The seller who opened that thread quotes Amazon's AWD FAQ as "The replenishments take up to an average of 14 days to move from an AWD center to the FBA network" [official, via a seller].

**E5. Amazon Managed Service.** [official] sell.amazon.com/programs/supply-chain claims "a 15% increase in unit sales on average" for Amazon Managed Service (end to end), footnoted "*Calculated April - June 2023". It is the same number as the MIL claim in C1; neither page gives a method.

### F. The lower bound: the low-inventory-level fee (US)

Amounts and the stock-out side belong to `areas/running-out.md`; what bounds a restock plan is the definition.

**F1. Until 2027-01-31.** [official] News_Amazon 2023-12-19 and Bryce_Amazon 2023-12-07: from 2024-04-01 the fee applies when both the long-term (90-day) and short-term (30-day) historical days of supply are below 28 days. Bryce_Amazon 2023-12-07 (verifier re-read 2026-11-10) adds the level it was computed at then: "The historical days of supply metric is calculated at the parent-product level." **F2a. The method in Amazon's own words (Chinese notice).** [official] Amazon Global Selling (China), "2027年亚马逊美国站销售佣金和亚马逊物流费用变更", https://globalselling.amazon.com/news/news-notics-261105 (verifier GET 2026-11-10; the page prints the date 2026年10月5日 though its slug and content are the 2026-11-05 announcement): "亚马逊每周按FNSKU评估库存，并记录库存评估日的库存数量，分两次计算： 第一次，在评估日计算： 用当天记录的库存数量，除以评估日前28天的日均发货量。 第二次，在评估日28天后计算： 仍用评估日记录的同一库存数量，除以评估日后28天的日均发货量。 … 只有两次结果都不足28天，且符合政策规定的收费条件，才会在第二次评估完成后收费。" (Amazon assesses inventory weekly per FNSKU and records the quantity on the assessment date; first computation on that date: that quantity ÷ average daily shipments in the 28 days before; second, 28 days later: the same quantity ÷ average daily shipments in the 28 days after; the fee is charged only if both are under 28 days and the policy's charging conditions are met, and only after the second assessment.) Its advice line, "建议始终保持4周库存水平" (we suggest always keeping four weeks of inventory), is Amazon's suggestion, not a term of the fee. The detail pages it links, G201411300 and GFU8NTVGHP2JQLCR, were not read (script shell).

**F2b. Further mechanics, seen only through a third party.** The following are not in the letter, the News post or the Chinese notice; they are PPC Land's reading of the unread help page GFU8NTVGHP2JQLCR. [official, via Luis Rijo, PPC Land, 2026-11-06], https://ppc.land/amazon-sellers-face-average-0-19-fba-fee-rise-from-january-15-2027/: checked weekly per FNSKU (also in F2a); trailing days of supply = inventory ÷ average daily units shipped in the 28 days before; following days of supply = the same inventory ÷ average daily units shipped in the 28 days after; the fee applies only if both are below 28 and "the rate is set by the higher of the two figures"; inventory counts sellable US FBA units including FC transfer, FC processing and staging, and excludes unsellable (pending removal, disposal or liquidation) and inbound units; shipped units include FBA, Multi-Channel Fulfillment, Remote Fulfillment "and any other program that ships units from our US fulfillment network", and "Seller-fulfilled inventory does not count"; "The fee is charged per unit, but only on the units shipped in the first week after the inventory check, which Amazon calls 'following week 1'"; the charge appears four weeks after the assessed week. Also relayed (verifier re-read 2026-11-10): from 2027-02-01 "the fee will now apply to items in all oversize product size tiers"; it moves out of the fulfillment fee line to "a standalone fee on the Payment view report"; a new Low-Inventory-Level Fee Report from 2027-02-01 gives order-level detail with trailing and following days of supply and any exemption reason; the FAQ: "As long as your inventory levels are greater than your units shipped in the trailing 28-days, you won't be charged … Even if your demand spikes, the fee won't apply." Exemptions as relayed: new Professional sellers for 365 days; new-to-FBA parent products for 120 days or the first 200 units in FBA New Selection; SKUs with at least 70% of inventory auto-replenished via AWD over the prior 90 days; products shipping fewer than 20 units in the past 7 days; grocery.

**F3. What the 2027 test changes for a plan.** Inbound units do not count toward the 28 days in either version (2027 wording via PPC Land), so a shipment on the water does not lift a SKU out of the fee; only received, sellable units do. The two-sided test means a SKU that runs low because demand jumped (following shipments high, trailing low) is charged only if it was also under 28 days on the trailing rate, which is what Amazon's FAQ sentence says. [seat for the second sentence; the FAQ as relayed by PPC Land supports it] Because the following figure needs four weeks of shipments, the fee for a given week is knowable only four weeks later; the trailing figure is the half a seller can compute at the check.

**F4. Outside the US.** Not established. The helper pass did not reach EU or Japan (Q8).

### I. What the sales reads count

**I1. `getOrderMetrics`** [official] local model `sales.json`: `unitCount` is "The number of units in orders based on the specified filters." It is ordered units; the model does not say how cancelled or pending orders are treated. One marketplace per call; `granularityTimeZone` is required above Hour; Hour granularity reaches 30 days back, other granularities two years; filters `fulfillmentNetwork` (MFN/AFN), `buyerType` (B2B/B2C/All), `asin` or `sku` (not both).

**I2. Sales and Traffic** [official] `sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json` and Data Kiosk `analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24`: `unitsOrdered` ("The number of units ordered."), `unitsOrderedB2B`, `unitsShipped`, `unitsRefunded`; `asinGranularity` PARENT / CHILD / SKU with default PARENT; weeks run Sunday to Saturday; start date at most two years back.

**I3. Planning and Restock reports** count shipped units by name (`units-shipped-t7…t90`), and `Units Sold Last 30 Days` in the Restock report is undefined on the page read.

**I4. Shipment-level** `GET_FBA_FULFILLMENT_CUSTOMER_SHIPMENT_SALES_DATA` [official] developer-docs: "shipped FBA customer orders … near real-time"; the page's general note says the delay is "approximately one to three hours … In some rare cases … up to 24 hours".

**I5. Which base Amazon's own tests use.** Both versions of the low-inventory-level fee and historical days of supply are computed on *shipped* units (C3, F2), and the 2027 version counts Multi-Channel and Remote Fulfillment shipments. A velocity built on ordered units from one marketplace is a different number from the one Amazon tests.

## What practitioners add

### P1. The common core: target days × velocity − stock position

Every tool read computes the same shape; they differ in what goes into each term.

- **RestockPro (eComEngine)** [opinion], https://www.ecomengine.com/help/suggested-quantity-calculation: Coverage Required = (target quantity-on-hand days + lead time) × velocity; Suggested Reorder = Coverage Required − (quantity on hand at Amazon + local + on order); the page's example: "(45 + 14) x 1.2 = 70.8 units". The 30/45 starting point is on its Restock Rules page, https://www.ecomengine.com/help/restock-rules: "Most sellers start with a minimum of 30 days here and a maximum of 45, but you can adjust up or down anytime."
- **Inventory Planner (Sage)** [opinion], https://help.inventory-planner.com/en/articles/1959506-how-is-the-forecast-and-replenishment-calculated: planning period = lead time + days of stock; replenishment = forecast need over that period − (stock + on order + in transfer).
- **InventoryLab** [opinion; page "Restock Report - Calculations | Insights" on the UseResponse host `thjrecvolts.useresponse.com`, which links to inventorylab.com/insights and InventoryLab user guides; re-fetched 2026-11-10]: "Reorder Point (this is in-stock quantity, not a date) = Lead Time Usage (Avg. Daily Sales * Lead Time) + Safety Stock … Safety Stock = Avg Daily Sales x Lead Time Average Daily Sales = Avg. Daily Sales of the past 30 days"; only fulfillable on-hand units enter the formula; "Restock Soon" defaults to 14 days (these two not re-found in the re-fetch).
- **Helium 10 Inventory Management** [opinion], https://kb.helium10.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057872693 (updated 2023-07-06): "Lead Time is the number of days that it takes your suppliers to manufacture and ship your products (Helium 10 defaults this to 25 days). Reorder Frequency is the number of days of inventory you want ordered (Helium 10 defaults this to 30 days)."
- **Jungle Scout Inventory Manager** [opinion], blog dated 2021-08-03: statuses Reorder Now / Reorder Soon / Overstock / In Stock; "Date to Reorder: The latest date to reorder your products, in order to avoid a stockout"; average daily sales over a seller-chosen "Sales History" range.
- **领星** [opinion], https://www.lingxing.com/help/article/replenishmentAadvice: "建议采购量（普通模式） = 备货时长 × 日销量（动态）-（FBA库存+FBA在途+海外仓在途+海外仓可用+本地可用+待检量+待交付+本地仓在途+采购计划）" with "备货时长=采购计划+采购交期+质检天数+本地仓至FBA时效+安全天数+补货频率" (stocking duration = purchase-planning days + supplier lead time + QC days + local-to-FBA transit + safety days + replenishment frequency). The page's own caveat: "以上计算公式不建议直接套用数值计算，需要考虑在途等数据是否能在备货时长内到达" (do not plug numbers straight in; whether in-transit stock arrives inside the stocking duration must be considered).
- **赛狐** [opinion], https://www.sellfox.com/help/features.html?id=359: "建议补货量 = (备货天数 + 安全天数) * 日均销量 - (FBA库存 + FBA在途 + AWD库存 + AWD在途) - (海外仓可用 + 海外仓占用 + 海外仓在途 + 海外调拨在途)".
- **积加** [opinion], https://help.jijiaerp.com/docs/eeE2AHAR (modified 2025-09-19): "目标库存天数 = 备货计划与审批时间 + 采购交期 + 质检时间 + 物流方式(天) + 入库处理天数 + 海外仓调拨天数（开启）+ 安全库存天数 + 补货周期 - 缺货天数"; its stated ideal: "仓库内的所有库存刚好用完的时候，下一批采购的物资正好到货" (the next purchase lands exactly as stock runs out).
- **优麦云** [opinion], https://www.sellerspace.com/help/invetory-analysis-guide (2025-03-31): "建议发货量 = (头程 + 安全天数) * 日销量 - （可售数量 + 入库数量 + 调查数量 + 调拨预留 + 处理预留）"; it counts units under investigation as stock.
- **马帮** and **易仓**: help centres unreachable; the formulas seen are [snippet] and unverified. 易仓's own 2021-10-09 article [opinion] gives "采购周期+到货天数+备货天数*日均销量-FBA库存" as printed, parentheses missing.

Chinese tools separate two quantities that most English tools merge: 建议采购量 (what to buy from the factory, netting local and supplier-side stock) and 建议发货量 (what to ship to FBA now, netting only FBA-side stock), each with its own day count.

### P3. Stock-out correction

A day with no stock records no demand, so a plain average understates velocity. Variants read:

- Denominator minus stock-out days: 领星 ("天数=历史时间段天数-断货天数"), 赛狐 ("x天日均 = x天日销量/(x-n)"; a stock-out day is one where FBA stock of 0 made sales 0; example 100 units over 7 days with 3 out → 25 a day), 积加, Inventory Planner (150 units in 31 days: 4.84 a day raw, 9.38 after removing stock-out days), sellerboard.
- SoStocked: a stock-out day is zero inventory and sales below 30% of average; the days are replaced or removed.
- RestockPro (https://www.ecomengine.com/help/restock-rules): "RestockPro first attempts to find a similar in-stock time period on which to base the sales velocity. If it cannot, it will adjust the velocity up to 15% … This setting only looks at out of stock periods in the last 30 days."
- No correction named: 优麦云, Helium 10 (page read), Amazon's plugin.

The correction needs a daily stock history. `GET_LEDGER_SUMMARY_VIEW_DATA` (AF) is the report that carries daily ending balances; see `areas/stock-states.md`.

## Published research

### R-A. Reorder point and safety stock (gap fill 2026-11-10)

[research] Dennis Bricker, "Reorder Point" lecture slides, Dept. of Mechanical & Industrial Engineering, University of Iowa, https://user.engineering.uiowa.edu/~dbricker/Lecture_Slides/Reorder_Point.pdf (read 2026-11-10):
- Setting: "the inventory level is continuously monitored, a company places an order for Q units of product every time the inventory position drops to a reorder point R." "Inventory position: #stock-on-hand + #inventory on-order −#backorders". "Safety stock: (reorder point) −(expected lead-time demand)".
- "The re-order point is the sum of the expected lead-time demand and the safety stock. The safety stock is the product of the safety factor and the standard deviation of lead-time demand."
- Lead-time variability: with daily demand d ~ N(μ_d, σ_d) and random lead time T with mean μ_t and standard deviation σ_t, lead-time demand has mean μ_LT = μ_t·μ_d and standard deviation σ_LT = √(μ_t·σ_d² + μ_d²·σ_t²). "A longer lead time with no variability might require less safety stock, and therefore be more desirable than a shorter expected lead time with high variability!"
- What it assumes: normally distributed demand per period, independent of lead time; backorders or lost sales handled explicitly (the slides treat both cases in the fill-rate example).
- What it changes for a seller's arithmetic: safety stock grows with the square root of lead time for demand noise, and linearly with average demand for lead-time noise. A long ocean leg with variable port and receiving time (H1, P5) enters through σ_t, which a fixed "safety days" term (P9) does not separate.

[research] Chopra, Reinhardt and Dada, "The Effect of Lead Time Uncertainty on Safety Stocks", working paper dated 2003-07-31, https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/chopra/htm/research/lead-timejuly31-2003.pdf (read 2026-11-10): "Safety stock is a function of the cycle service level, demand uncertainty, the replenishment lead time, and the lead time uncertainty." The normal approximation "indicates that reducing lead time variability tends to have a greater impact than reducing lead times", but "the conclusions from the normal approximation are flawed, especially in the range of service levels where most companies operate. We show the existence of a service level threshold greater than 50 percent below which re-order points increase with a decrease in lead time variability." What it changes: whether shortening the lead time or making it more reliable cuts more stock depends on the service level aimed at; the normal-approximation formula above can mislead in that range.

### R-B. Cycle service level against fill rate (gap fill 2026-11-10)

[research] Bricker (above): "Cycle service level: percentage of cycles without a stockout. Product fill rate: percentage of product demand filled without backordering. These two measures can be quite different!" Worked example on the slides: with Q = 500, expected lead-time demand 150 and σ 50, a 99% fill rate needs a safety factor of 0.9 (R = 195), which "means that the cycle service level would be only 81.6%. (There would be a 18.4% probability of a stock-out in each cycle!)"

[research] MIT Center for Transportation & Logistics, CTL.SC1x Supply Chain & Logistics Fundamentals, "Key Concepts: Week 7 Lesson 1: Probabilistic Inventory Models", https://courses.edx.org/asset-v1:MITx+CTL.SC1x_1+2T2015+type@asset+block/KeyConcept_Week7Lesson1.pdf (read 2026-11-10): "The CSL is the probability that there will not be a stock out within a replenishment cycle"; "The IFR is the fraction of demand that is met with the inventory on hand out of cycle stock"; "Regardless of the metrics used, the end result is a safety factor, k, and a safety stock. The safety stock is simply kσDL. The term σDL is defined as the standard deviation of demand over lead time, but it is more technically the root mean square error (RMSE) of the forecast over the lead time." Chris Caplice, ESD.260 "Inventory Management: Probabilistic Demand", MIT, Oct 2006, https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/esd-260j-logistics-systems-fall-2006/8b53c45fd26ffff706d815131e8d177e_lect11.pdf (read 2026-11-10): "IFR usually much higher than CSL for same SS".

What the choice changes: a target stated as "never run out in a cycle" (CSL) buys more safety stock than the same number stated as a share of units served (fill rate), and the gap widens as orders grow larger relative to lead-time demand. Amazon's own figures in this file are neither: MIL is a unit floor (C1) and the fee tests days of supply (F).

### R-C. Periodic against continuous review (gap fill 2026-11-10)

[research] Caplice, ESD.260 (above): periodic review: "Know stock level only at certain times", "Ordering occurs at review", with "Coordination of replenishments", "Able to predict workload"; continuous review: "Replenishments made dynamically", "Able to provide same level of service with less safety stock". Policies named: (s, Q) "Order Q if IP ≤ s", (s, S) "Min-Max system", (R, S) "Order S-IP every R time periods", (R, s, S) hybrid.

[research] MIT CTL.SC1x, "Key Concepts: Week 7 Lesson 2", https://courses.edx.org/asset-v1:MITx+CTL.SC1x_1+2T2015+type@asset+block/KeyConcept_Week7Lesson2.pdf (read 2026-11-10): for (R, S) "the order up to point, S* is the sum of the expected demand over the lead-time and the replenishment time plus the RMSE of the forecast error over lead plus replenishment time multiplied by some safety factor k"; the conversion from (s, Q): "Q for continuous becomes D*R for periodic", "L for a continuous becomes R+L for periodic"; "Periodic Review policies are very popular because they fit the regular pattern of work where ordering might occur only once a week or once every two weeks."

What the choice changes: a review cycle R widens the period the safety stock must protect from L to R+L. Amazon's restock settings include "Replenishment frequency" (A4) and the Chinese ERPs add 补货频率 / 补货周期 to the stocking days (P1); both are the R of a periodic policy. A tool that re-plans every day with no cycle term behaves as continuous review.

### R-D. Intermittent demand: Croston and TSB (gap fill 2026-11-10)

[research] Teunter, Syntetos and Babai, "Intermittent demand: Linking forecasting to inventory obsolescence", European Journal of Operational Research 214(3), 2011, 606–615, abstract at https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ejores:v:214:y:2011:i:3:p:606-615 (read 2026-11-10): "The standard method to forecast intermittent demand is that by Croston … It uses exponential smoothing to separately update the estimated demand size and demand interval whenever a positive demand occurs, and their ratio provides the forecast of demand per period. The Croston method has two important disadvantages. First and foremost, not updating after (many) periods with zero demand renders the method unsuitable for dealing with obsolescence issues. Second, the method is positively biased … The second issue has been addressed in the literature by the proposal of an estimator (Syntetos-Boylan Approximation, SBA) that is approximately unbiased." Their method (TSB) "is unbiased (for all points in time) and it updates the demand probability instead of the demand interval, doing so in every period." Croston's original paper (1972) was not opened; Croston is cited here through this abstract.

What it assumes and changes: a slow seller with many zero days breaks a plain daily average. Croston forecasts the size and the gap separately and holds its forecast through zero days; TSB lowers the forecast through zero days. On Amazon a zero day can also be a stock-out day (P3), which neither method distinguishes by itself.

### R-E. Retail forecasting at scale: the M5 competition (gap fill 2026-11-10)

[research] Makridakis, Spiliotis and Assimakopoulos, "M5 accuracy competition: Results, findings, and conclusions", International Journal of Forecasting 38(4), 2022, 1346–1364 (abstract https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intfor:v:38:y:2022:i:4:p:1346-1364; preprint of October 2020 read at https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/M5_accuracy_competition.pdf, 2026-11-10). The task: "42,840 time series representing the hierarchical unit sales for the largest retail company in the world by revenue, Walmart", daily data with a 28-day horizon, "focusing on series that display intermittency". The preprint's findings, quoted: "M5 is, therefore, the first competition where all top-performing methods were both 'pure' ML ones and significantly better than all statistical benchmarks"; "The value of combining"; "The value of 'cross-learning'" (one model across many related series); "the winning submissions … were also, on average, more than 20% better in terms of WRMSSE. Although the differences were smaller at lower aggregation levels"; "all winning submissions utilized external information", with "several price-related features … of significant importance", and an exponential-smoothing benchmark given promotion and event variables "6% better" than the same without. On intermittency: at the three lowest (product-store) levels Croston's method beat seasonal naive "by 13.0%, 20.2%, and 27.0%", while seasonal naive was 11.5% more accurate on average across levels. Of the participating teams, "415 (7.5%) beat the top-performing benchmark (ES bu)" (preprint figures; the journal version's figures were not read).

What it changes, as a choice: the gains are largest when many related series are pooled and price, promotion and calendar are inputs; at single-SKU daily level the gap to simple methods narrows. A seller's per-SKU weighted average (P2) is a single-series, no-covariate method.

### R-F. Amazon's own published forecasting work (gap fill 2026-11-10)

[research] Salinas, Flunkert and Gasthaus, "DeepAR: Probabilistic Forecasting with Autoregressive Recurrent Networks", arXiv:1704.04110 (v3 2019-02-22), https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110 (read 2026-11-10): "In retail businesses, for example, forecasting demand is crucial for having the right inventory available at the right time at the right place … we propose DeepAR, a methodology for producing accurate probabilistic forecasts, based on training an auto regressive recurrent network model on a large number of related time series … accuracy improvements of around 15% compared to state-of-the-art methods."

[research] Wen, Torkkola, Narayanaswamy and Madeka, "A Multi-Horizon Quantile Recurrent Forecaster", arXiv:1711.11053, NIPS 2017 Time Series Workshop, https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11053 (read 2026-11-10): sequence-to-sequence networks with quantile regression and direct multi-horizon output; "accommodates both temporal and static covariates, learning across multiple related series, shifting seasonality, future planned event spikes and cold-starts", demonstrated on "the future demand of items sold on" Amazon.

[official] Amazon Science, "The history of Amazon's forecasting algorithm", staff writer, 2021-08-09, https://www.amazon.science/latest-news/the-history-of-amazons-forecasting-algorithm (read 2026-11-10): the approach "known as MQ-RNN/CNN, was published in a 2018 paper titled 'A Multi-Horizon Quantile Recurrent Forecaster'"; its successor "MQTransformer … published in December 2020", after which "Amazon now has a unified forecasting model".

What this does and does not establish: Amazon forecasts quantiles of demand, not a single number, and its connector metric names carry P80 and P90 forecasts (D2). No Amazon page read says which model drives a seller's Restock recommendation or MIL; these papers describe Amazon's retail forecasting, and linking them to the seller tools is not established.

### R-G. Demand censored by stock-outs (gap fill 2026-11-10)

[research] Jain, Rudi and Wang, "Demand Estimation and Ordering Under Censoring: Stock-Out Timing Is (Almost) All You Need", Operations Research 63(1), 2015, 134–150, abstract https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:oropre:v:63:y:2015:i:1:p:134-150 (read 2026-11-10): "when demand exceeds inventory (i.e., when a stock-out event occurs), a retailer in general cannot observe actual demand. We propose using observations on the timing of sales occurrences in a Bayesian fashion to learn about demand … the optimal order quantity with timing observations is greater than the optimal order quantity with full demand observations … the use of timing observations eliminates 76.1%" of the expected profit loss relative to uncensored observations (abstract, truncated in the read).

[research] Trapero, Holgado de Frutos and Pedregal, "Demand forecasting under lost sales stock policies", International Journal of Forecasting 40(3), 2024, 1055–1068, abstract https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intfor:v:40:y:2024:i:3:p:1055-1068 (read 2026-11-10): "when the stock policy follows a lost sales context and the demand is estimated by means of sales data … forecasting models should use censored demand estimations. Unfortunately, the literature about censored demand forecasting remains very limited, without an accepted general solution for this problem." They propose a Tobit Kalman filter.

What it changes: sales on a day the SKU ran out are a floor on demand, not demand. The tools' fixes (drop stock-out days, cap the lift, P3) are one family of answers; the literature's are likelihood or Bayesian estimates that use the censoring point or the time of the stock-out. The timing approach needs intraday sales and stock, which the ledger (R7) and order reports give only at day or order level.

Not found or not opened: Croston (1972) itself; a published study of Amazon sellers' restocking or of the Restock tool's accuracy; newsvendor material beyond the MIT key-concepts sheet's one line ("Order Q* at start of period where P[x ≤ Q]=CR").

## The seller's choices

Each is the seller's. What each changes is stated; none is picked.

1. **Which velocity.** A short window follows a turn in demand quickly and swings with a promotion or a stock-out; a long window is steady and late. Weighted mixes (50/30/20 over 7/14/30 at 领星 and 赛狐; 40/30/20/10 over 3/7/14/30 at 优麦云; seven equal windows at SoStocked; a plain 7 days at 积加; a plain 30 at Amazon's plugin) are different answers to the same trade.
2. **Ordered or shipped units; one marketplace or the pool; with or without Multi-Channel, B2B and removal orders.** Amazon's fee test uses shipped units including Multi-Channel and Remote Fulfillment. A pooled stock (Pan-EU, NARF) netted against one marketplace's demand overstates cover.
3. **Whether to correct for stock-out days, and how.** Removing them raises velocity; RestockPro caps the lift at 15%, others do not cap it.
4. **Whether and how to carry seasonality**: last year × ratio, a single coefficient, a dated target, or nothing; and whether promotion days are kept, flattened or dropped.
5. **Lead time: one number or stages, average or worst case, and which freight mode.** Planning on the slowest mode (领星's default) orders earliest and holds most; planning on air holds least and costs most per unit. Whether Amazon's receiving time is its own stage changes the total by days to weeks (H1).
6. **Review rhythm.** Ordering on a fixed cycle (replenishment frequency, 补货周期) adds the cycle length to the cover each order must buy; ordering whenever a reorder point is crossed needs no cycle term and produces irregular, smaller orders.
7. **How much safety cover, and where it sits.** Days at FBA count toward Amazon's days of supply, storage and capacity; units held back in the seller's warehouse, a 3PL or AWD do not, and reach FBA only after a transfer time.
8. **Target cover between Amazon's two lines.** Below 28 historical days the US fee can apply; above 90 forecast days Amazon calls the stock excess, and age-based storage rises with time. Practitioners' bands (RestockPro 30–45, sellerboard 35–55, SoStocked's 35-day buffer) are theirs.
9. **What to do when nothing can land in time**: accept the gap and drop the lost demand from the order (领星, 店小秘, 积加), pay for air, or slow sales; and whether price is a lever at all.
10. **Rounding** to supplier minimums and cartons: up buys cover and cash; down risks the gap.
11. **Amazon-managed or seller-managed.** AWD auto-replenishment removes the capacity limit and the fee for that stock and hands Amazon the quantity and timing (E3).
12. **How far to trust Amazon's recommendation.** It sees Amazon's forecast and the seller's keyed settings; it does not see the factory, a second warehouse, or cash (P10).
13. **New products**, with no history: a manual velocity (SoStocked), category seasonality (Inventory Planner), or a launch quantity by judgement. 赛狐 and 领星 show a product only once it has FBA stock or sales.
14. **Which service measure, if any** (R-B): a chance of no stock-out per cycle, a share of units served, a days-of-cover target, or Amazon's unit floor (MIL). Each implies a different safety stock for the same data.
15. **Which forecaster** (R-D, R-E, R-F): a weighted moving average, a Croston-type method for slow sellers, a pooled model with price and promotion inputs, or Amazon's own output taken as given. Each assumes something different about zero days, promotions and related items.
16. **How to treat stock-out days** (P3, R-G): drop them, cap a lift, or estimate the censored demand.

- **P-restock-and-forecast-48.** Reorder point = expected lead-time demand + safety factor × standard deviation of lead-time demand; with random lead time that deviation is √(μ_t·σ_d² + μ_d²·σ_t²), so lead-time variability and demand variability enter separately. - **P-restock-and-forecast-49.** Cycle service level (no stock-out in a cycle) and fill rate (share of demand served from stock) are different targets; in a textbook example a 99% fill rate corresponds to an 81.6% cycle service level. Source: Bricker; MIT CTL.SC1x; MIT ESD.260 [research]. §R-B.

- **P-restock-and-forecast-51.** Croston's method forecasts intermittent demand from separately smoothed demand size and interval, updates only after a positive demand and is positively biased; TSB updates a demand probability every period and lowers the forecast through zero periods.
