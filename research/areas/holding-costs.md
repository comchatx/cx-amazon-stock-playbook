## Amazon's own rules

Unless a marketplace is named, a statement in sections A–I is about the United States store.

### A. Monthly inventory storage fee

A1. **What it is charged on.** The fee is charged per calendar month on the daily average volume the seller's units occupy in fulfilment centres. The clearest Amazon wording opened this session is Europe's: "Monthly storage fees are charged for all units stored in an Amazon fulfilment centre based on calendar month and your daily average volume (measured in cubic feet for the United Kingdom and cubic metres for the other European stores). The measurement of any unit will be based on the unit's size when properly packaged and ready for shipping to customers in accordance with the FBA programme policies. Monthly storage fees consist of base monthly storage fee, which is determined by product size and time of year, and storage utilisation surcharge, which is in addition to the base monthly storage fee." [official] — "Rate Card — Europe Fees — Effective as of 2 November, 2026", p.21, https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/02/sell/images/261014-FBA-Rate-Card-EN.pdf, read 2026-11-10. Canada's public page says the same in its own unit: "Inventory storage fees are charged monthly based on the daily average volume (measured in cubic metres) for the space your inventory occupies in Amazon fulfilment centres. The volume measurement is based on unit size when properly packaged and ready to ship." [official] — sell.amazon.ca/pricing, read 2026-11-10. The US help page "Monthly inventory storage fee" (G3EDYEF6KUCFQTNM) was unreachable; the US wording is not quoted here.

A2. **Amazon's measurement governs.** "Amazon will be entitled to make its own measurement of the cubic metre or weight of any packaged unit … Amazon's measurement will govern." [official] — EU rate card 2 Nov 2026, FAQ Q2, read 2026-11-10. The US equivalent sentence was not read.

A3. **What the report shows, and so what the computation uses.** The SP-API report `GET_FBA_STORAGE_FEE_CHARGES_DATA` "Contains estimated monthly inventory storage fees for each ASIN of a seller's inventory in Amazon fulfillment centers." Its columns are, in order: `asin, fnsku, product_name, fulfillment_center, country_code, longest_side, median_side, shortest_side, measurement_units, weight, weight_units, item_volume, volume_units, product_size_tier, average_quantity_on_hand, average_quantity_pending_removal, estimated_total_item_volume, month_of_charge, storage_rate, storage_utilization_ratio, storage_utilization_ratio_units, base_rate, utilization_surcharge_rate, avg_qty_for_sus, est_vol_for_sus, est_base_msf, est_sus, currency, estimated_monthly_storage_fee, dangerous_goods_storage_type, eligible_for_inventory_discount, qualifies_for_inventory_discount, total_incentive_fee_amount, breakdown_incentive_fee_amount, average_quantity_customer_orders`. [official] — "FBA Reports" (report type values), https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-fba.md, page `updatedAt` 2026-10-13, read 2026-11-10. The grain is FNSKU × fulfilment centre × month. The report carries the base rate and the surcharge rate separately (`base_rate`, `utilization_surcharge_rate`), the quantity and volume the surcharge was applied to separately from the quantity the base fee was applied to (`avg_qty_for_sus`, `est_vol_for_sus` beside `average_quantity_on_hand`, `estimated_total_item_volume`), and a separate average for units pending removal (`average_quantity_pending_removal`). The column list is Amazon's; that `avg_qty_for_sus` is smaller than `average_quantity_on_hand` because of the 0–30-day exclusion in B3 is [seat].

A4. **Seasons and rate classes.** Every marketplace read prices storage in two seasons, January–September and October–December, and by size class; Europe and the US add a dangerous-goods class, and Europe and Japan a clothing-type class (J). For the US the only rate read in Amazon's own words this session is the 2024 change: non-peak standard-size storage was lowered from an average of $0.87 to $0.78 per cubic foot from 2024-04-01 [official] — "Update to US referral and Fulfillment by Amazon fees" (2024 letter, dated 2023-12-05), sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com, read 2026-11-10. The US October–December standard rate ($2.40 per cubic foot), the oversize rates and the dangerous-goods rates were seen only in third-party pages [opinion: EcomCrew; Nova] and are not stated here as Amazon's. The live rate is in the report's `storage_rate` and `base_rate` columns (R1).

A5. **When it is charged.** The fee for a month is charged in the following month. The window "between the 7th and 15th of the month following the month for which the fee applies" was seen for the US only as a search snippet of the help page [snippet] and in a third-party paraphrase of the 2027 page ("charged 7th–15th of following month") [opinion: Seller Essentials]. Europe's surcharge wording agrees on the direction: "If any of your storage utilisation ratios are above 26 weeks on May 31, 2023, you'll incur the surcharge in June." [official, via a seller pasting Amazon's e-mail in Amazon's EU forum] — https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/4271e2a69f2700d7b9eefd246708727c, read 2026-11-09.

A6. **What keeps paying and what stops.** Stranded stock keeps paying storage: Amazon staff, 2026-10-06 [official, staff post, Kris_Amazon] — https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/3f8ddf26-6255-42da-bfa8-bbe14db34d57, read 2026-11-10. "Once a removal order is created, storage fees stop during the processing period until completion." [official, staff post, Angie_Amazon, 2026-02-09] — https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/85f60e98-f93c-48fe-bee7-07917616f827, read 2026-11-10. The same staff account says once a liquidation order is submitted, storage and the aged surcharge stop accruing (Kris_Amazon, 2026-10-06, same thread as above). Both are staff posts; the policy page that would confirm them was unreachable, and the storage report's separate `average_quantity_pending_removal` column is consistent with pending-removal units being counted apart. An earlier staff reply says the same of the aged surcharge: "these aged inventory fees are based on the average number of units stored in FBA per month, minus average number of units pending removal" [official, staff post, Jameson_Amazon, thread opened 2024-04-25] — https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/a0487a8d-27ec-4703-bdd0-4b0e906f741c, read 2026-11-10 by the verifier.

A7. **Dated changes, US.** 2023: off-peak storage up $0.04 per cubic foot for standard-size and $0.03 for oversize; peak up $0.20 for non-sortable [official, News post created 2022-11-17] — "2023 US referral and FBA fee changes", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/8223d913aba4b2e9e5239c3e121d7fa8, read 2026-11-10; the letter's general effective date is 2023-01-17, and the storage date (third parties say 2023-02-01) is not in the text as read. 2024-04-01: non-peak standard-size cut to $0.78 (A4). 2025: "no increases" and no new fee types (2025 letter, 2024-11-19) [official]. 2026: an average $0.08 per unit increase on fulfilment, no new fee types, effective 2026-01-15 (2026 letter, 2025-10-15) [official]; no storage rate change was read. 2027-02-01: the fee becomes an age-based rate (section I).

### B. Storage utilization surcharge (US, until 2027-01-31)

B1. **Introduction.** "We will introduce a storage utilization surcharge for sellers who have a high cube of inventory stored in our fulfillment centers relative to the cube of their recent weekly sales." "This change will take effect on April 1, 2023." Amazon estimated it would affect about 7.5% of sellers. [official, News post created 2022-11-17] — URL in A7, read 2026-11-10.

B2. **The ratio.** Amazon's fullest definition read this session is in the European rate card: "this surcharge is based on storage utilisation ratio, which is the ratio of your average daily inventory volume stored divided by the average daily shipped volume during the past 13 weeks." "The ratio is calculated on the last day of a given month as the average daily inventory volume stored divided by the average daily shipped volume over the past 13 weeks. … the surcharge will be added to your base monthly storage fee and applied to the daily average volume for the space that your inventory occupies in fulfilment centres." [official] — EU rate card 2 Nov 2026, p.21 and footnote 3, read 2026-11-10. The ratio is in weeks. It is a seller-level number per size class, not a per-SKU one: "you may have a storage utilisation ratio for standard-size products …, and a different storage utilisation ratio for oversize products" (same page). The US report carries the ratio per row in `storage_utilization_ratio` with `storage_utilization_ratio_units` (A3).

B3. **Threshold, tiers, exemptions: what was and was not read for the US.** The US help page (G3EDYEF6KUCFQTNM / GJ9NNG7RK4TU6E3Z) was unreachable. What is established: the US threshold in 2026 is 22 weeks and stock aged 0–30 days is outside the surcharge, from Amazon's Supply Chain Portal wording captured by the US helper [official, via helper's capture; not re-opened by this seat]; the exemptions "new sellers", Individual accounts and "less than 25 cubic feet of daily volume" are Amazon's words in the **European** card (J2) and are repeated for the US only by third parties [opinion: Inventory Hero; Goat Consulting; eComEngine]. The US tier table (the per-cubic-foot rate for each band of weeks, by size class and season) was not read from any Amazon page. Third-party pages disagree on whether the US threshold is 22 or 26 weeks, sometimes on one page (P9); the 26-week figure was the 2023 threshold and is still Japan's (J6).

B4. **New Selection.** "No low-inventory-level fee or storage utilization surcharge on your first 200 units for 120 days" for eligible new parent ASINs [official, News post 2026-10-28] — https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/0350c06a-2b11-4a24-b489-f608fb83fc47, read 2026-11-10.

B5. **Announced end.** "Effective February 1, 2027, we'll eliminate the storage utilization surcharge, which will make it easier to stock up ahead of high-demand events." [official] — "Update to US Referral and Fulfillment by Amazon fees for 2027", Sunny Jain, dated 2026-11-05, https://sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com/update-to-us-referral-and-fulfillment-by-amazon-fees-for-2027, read 2026-11-10. Canada's announcement of the same day says the same (J4).

### C. Aged inventory surcharge (US, until 2027-01-31)

C1. **Name.** The aged inventory surcharge is the fee "previously known as the Long-term Storage Fee" [official] — Data Kiosk schema `analytics_economics_2024_03_15.graphql`, `PreviewFeeType.AGED_INVENTORY_SURCHARGE`, local file (selling-partner-api-models), read 2026-11-10. The report type and its Brazil columns keep the old name (C8, L).

C2. **Assessment and charge dates.** "assessed monthly on the 15th of each month and charged between the 18th-22nd" [official, staff post, Angie_Amazon, 2026-02-09] — URL in A6. The surcharge is in addition to the monthly storage fee (Europe's card: "This is in addition to regular inventory storage fees" [official]).

C3. **Buckets and rates in force from 2026-01-16.** Per the same staff post, "effective January 16, 2026": 181–210 days $0.50 per cubic foot; 211–240 $1.00; 241–270 $1.50 (these three exclude clothing, shoes, bags, jewelry and watches); 271–300 $5.45; 301–330 $5.70; 331–365 $5.90; 366–455 days $6.90 per cubic foot or $0.30 per unit, whichever is greater; 456 days or more $7.90 per cubic foot or $0.35 per unit, whichever is greater. [official, staff post] The 2026 fee summary page (G201411300) as screenshotted on Sellers Ask Sellers gives the same two top bands and says the 12–15-month per-unit minimum rose from $0.15 to $0.30 [official, via sellersasksellers.com/t/…/6593]; the listing seat read G201411300 v47 on 2026-10-14 with matching substance (not re-read). Rates are Amazon's and change yearly; the live rate per row is `rate-surcharge` in the report (R2).

C4. **Per-cubic-foot versus per-unit.** Below 366 days the charge is volume × rate. From 366 days it is the greater of the volume charge and a per-unit charge, so a small item pays the per-unit figure. This is where practitioners most often misstate the rule (P7).

C5. **Age is first-in, first-out across the network.** "Amazon calculates inventory age on a first-in, first-out basis across the entire fulfillment network — so items sold or removed are deducted from the oldest inventory first." [official, staff post, Kris_Amazon, 2026-10-06] — URL in A6. An earlier staff reply says the same and spells out the consequence: "Items that are sold or removed are deducted from the inventory that has been in the fulfillment network the longest, regardless of which unit was actually shipped or removed. For example, if an associate picks and ships a unit that arrived in the fulfillment center only recently, we will still deduct that unit from the oldest inventory in stock." [official, staff post, Jameson_Amazon, thread opened 2024-04-25, URL in A6]. Glenn_Amazon (2026-09-02, staff post) is cited for the same point in the US helper's log and was not re-opened by the verifier. The age is an accounting age, not the age of the unit a customer receives.

C6. **What stops the charge.** "Submit a removal or disposal order before the cleanup date (deadline: 11:59 PM PT on the 14th of each month). Once submitted, you won't incur aged inventory surcharges on those units, even if they aren't physically removed." Removal "may take up to 14 business days". [official, staff post, Angie_Amazon, 2026-02-09]. A second staff post (Indy_Amazon, 2026-09-19, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/d2757daf-a218-4573-91e5-0ec01b6f2ab7) repeats the 15th and the 14th 11:59 PM PT rule. So the rule is about the order's submission time, not the units' departure. Automated removal settings exist for stock older than 365 days and for stock with no sale in six or more months and older than 180 days (same Angie_Amazon post). Automated removal is not the only exit Amazon applies: an Amazon moderator wrote, in a reply shown "4 months ago" on 2026-11-10 (so about July 2026), that "the 'Disabled' setting on Automated Fulfillable Inventory Settings means you opted out of the automated removal service, but Amazon's policy still enforces mandatory removal for sellable inventory aged over 270 days, with the disposition method determined by Amazon" [official, staff post, Arish_Amazon] — https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/ed6c2c0c-4306-41e9-a6e5-59f51d1983ee, read 2026-11-10 by the verifier; `areas/stuck-stock.md` §C4 owns this and dates the US rule from 2025-10-26 on a seller-quoted Amazon e-mail. This sits beside Amazon's own US surcharge schedule, which still prices sellable stock from 271 to 456+ days (C3); the two Amazon texts are recorded together and neither is picked (see *Gap fills*, M). What resets age (whether removed units sent back start at day 0) was not read in Amazon's words this session (the verifier's search found no Amazon text either); practitioners say first-in-first-out means a top-up does not make old units young (P8), and `areas/stuck-stock.md` owns removal mechanics.

C7. **Exemptions.** The three bands from 181 to 270 days exclude clothing, shoes, bags, jewelry and watches (C3). The New Selection program gives "free storage" on the first 200 units for 120 days (B4). No other exemption was read in Amazon's words for the US.

C8. **Dated changes, US.** 2023-04-15: "we will increase the aged inventory surcharge … for inventory stored between 271 to 365 days" and add new surcharges "on inventory stored between 180-270 days, excluding… Apparel, Shoes, Bags, Jewelry, and Watches… effective for the April 15, 2023 aged inventory assessment date" [official, News post created 2022-11-17]. 2024-02-15: a change to the bands and rates is reported by third parties and was not read on an Amazon page (open question). 2026-01-16: the 366–455 and 456+ split with per-unit minimums $0.30 and $0.35 (C3). 2026: removal and disposal of standard-size aged items under 0.5 lb $0.20 cheaper [official, via sellersasksellers screenshot of G201411300]. 2027-02-01: "We'll also remove the aged inventory surcharge and combine it with monthly storage fees into a single, age-based rate." [official, 2027 letter].

C9. **The report.** `GET_FBA_FULFILLMENT_LONGTERM_STORAGE_FEE_CHARGES_DATA` "Contains the charge data for long-term storage."; the span between `dataStartTime` and `dataEndTime` "should be equal to one month"; columns `snapshot-date, sku, fnsku, asin, product-name, condition, per-unit-volume, currency, volume-unit, country, qty-charged, amount-charged, surcharge-age-tier, rate-surcharge`. [official] — FBA Reports page, read 2026-11-10. One row is one FNSKU × age tier at one snapshot date.

C10. **The forward estimate.** `GET_FBA_INVENTORY_PLANNING_DATA` carries, per SKU, the age quantities (`inv-age-0-to-90-days`, `inv-age-91-to-180-days`, `inv-age-181-to-270-days`, `inv-age-271-to-365-days`, finer `inv-age-0-to-30-days`, `31-to-60`, `61-to-90`, `181-to-330`, `331-to-365`) and, for the next assessment, pairs `quantity-to-be-charged-ais-181-210-days` / `estimated-ais-181-210-days`, and the same for 211–240, 241–270, 271–300, 301–330, 331–365; then for the US, Germany, the UK, France, Italy and Spain only `inv-age-366-to-455-days`, `inv-age-456-plus-days`, `quantity-to-be-charged-ais-366-455-days`, `estimated-ais-366-455-days`, `quantity-to-be-charged-ais-456-plus-days`, `estimated-ais-456-plus-days`; and for the other stores `inv-age-365-plus-days`, `quantity-to-be-charged-ais-365-plus-days`, `estimated-ais-365-plus-days` ("not available in US, DE, UK, FR, IT, ES"). It also carries `estimated-storage-cost-next-month`, `Inventory age snapshot date`, `recommended-action`, `recommended-removal-quantity`, `estimated-cost-savings-of-recommended-actions`. Brazil returns a reduced set with the older `qty-to-be-charged-ltsf-6-mo`, `projected-ltsf-6-mo`, `qty-to-be-charged-ltsf-12-mo`, `estimated-ltsf-next-charge`. [official] — FBA Reports page, read 2026-11-10. `GET_FBA_RECOMMENDED_REMOVAL_DATA` "Identifies sellable items that will be 365 days old or older during the next Long-Term Storage cleanup event, if the report is generated within six weeks of the cleanup event date." [official, same page].

### D. Low-inventory-level fee (US)

D1. **Introduction.** The fee "will apply starting April 1, 2024" to standard-size products [official] — 2024 letter (2023-12-05), read 2026-11-10. Canada's letter gives the reason in Amazon's words: "The fee applies if you carry consistently low levels of inventory relative to unit sales, as this inhibits our ability to distribute products across our network, degrading delivery speeds and increasing our shipping costs. Sellers can avoid this fee by maintaining more than four weeks of inventory relative to sales." [official, News post] — "Update to Canada FBA fees starting February 5, 2024", https://sellercentral.amazon.ca/seller-forums/discussions/t/122a9a51… (captured `forum-ca-122a9a51`), read 2026-11-10.

D2. **It is a surcharge on the fulfilment fee, per unit shipped.** The schema names it "FBA Fulfillment Fee Low Level Inventory Surcharge" (`PreviewFeeType.LOW_INVENTORY_LEVEL_FEE`, help GV43F6S76Y9DHYRH) [official, local schema file]. It is charged on units shipped, by size tier and by how low the days of supply are; it is not a charge on stock held. It belongs in this file because it is the cost of holding too little.

D3. **The 2026 rule (in force until 2027-01-31).** From the help page "Low-inventory-level fee" (GV43F6S76Y9DHYRH, v29), read 2026-10-14 by the listing seat, not re-read: the fee applies only when both the long-term (last 90 days) and the short-term (last 30 days) historical days of supply are below 28 days; it is computed at FNSKU level from 2026-01-15 (before that at parent-product level); the bands are 0–14, 14–21 and 21–28 days; the inventory counted is sellable units including fulfilment-centre transfers and units in FC processing, and excludes units pending removal and inbound units; shipments counted include Multi-Channel Fulfillment and Remote Fulfillment; the metric is updated weekly. The 2027 letter confirms the method in one sentence: "Today, the fee is assessed using trailing 30- and 90-day sales." [official, read 2026-11-10]. The 2026 summary page adds that the fee is at FNSKU level, that Grocery is exempt, and that it applies to Small and Large Bulky tiers from 2026 [official, via sellersasksellers screenshot]. Europe's card states the same two-window rule in full (J2).

D4. **Exemptions, 2026.** From the same help page (listing seat, 2026-10-14): new Professional sellers for 365 days; parent ASINs in the New Selection program for 180 days; FNSKUs at least 70% auto-replenished from AWD over the last 90 days; products that shipped fewer than 20 units in the past 7 days; Grocery. Amazon's own pages disagree on the New Selection window: the help page v29 says 180 days; the News post of 2026-10-28 says "first 200 units for 120 days" (B4). Both are recorded; the later one is the 2026–2027 program text.

D5. **Dated changes since launch.** 2024-04-01: fee starts. 2024-05-02 (News post "Update on the low-inventory-level fee", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/00fd861e-fed1-454a-970b-9d3e8384eb6a, read 2026-11-10) [official]: April fees credited; the transition extended through May 14 with credits by May 31; "starting May 15, the low-inventory-level fee will not apply to products that have sold less than 20 units in the past 7 days"; fees caused by Amazon's own delays credited by the 15th of the following month; a time-bound exception around Prime Day 2024 for Prime-exclusive Lightning Deals and Best Deals (four weeks after). Canada's copy of the same post (https://sellercentral.amazon.ca/seller-forums/discussions/t/e4055d60-2cad-4a69-98a9-dab86f98f5a1, posted 2024-05-02, re-read 2026-11-10) says "less than 10 units in the past 7 days". 2026-01-15: the grain moves from parent product to FNSKU; Grocery exempt; Small and Large Bulky included. 2027-02-01: the method changes (I3).

D6. **Where a seller sees it.** In `GET_FBA_INVENTORY_PLANNING_DATA`: `historical-days-of-supply`, `Short term historical days of supply`, `Long term historical days of supply`, `Last updated date for Historical Days of Supply`, `fba-minimum-inventory-level`, `fba-inventory-level-health-status`, `Exempted from Low-Inventory-Level fee?`, `Low-Inventory-Level fee applied in current week?`, `Recommended ship-in quantity`, `Recommended ship-in date`, `Inventory Supply at FBA`, `Total Days of Supply (including units from open shipments)` [official, FBA Reports page]. In the Fee and Economics Preview report in Seller Central: "To include Low Inventory Level fees in your report be sure to select fulfillment base rate and surcharges" (help GQNVDV46U9JQ25HY v3, read 2026-10-14 by the listing seat, not re-read). Amazon publishes no SP-API report type for the fee's charges (the money playbook's `fulfilment-charges.md` found none; this seat found none in the FBA Reports page). A "Low-Inventory-Level Fee Report" from 2027-02-01 is reported by a third party only (I3).

### E. Capacity limits, Capacity Manager, overage fee, IPI

E1. **The system since 2023-03-01.** "Effective March 1, 2023, we're replacing the weekly restock limit and quarterly storage limit with a single monthly capacity limit per storage type to give you more predictability and control over your inventory." Limits are announced monthly via the Capacity Monitor with "estimated limits for the following two months"; "We'll set capacity limits and measure your inventory usage by volume" (cubic feet). [official, News post] — "Simplifying FBA capacity limits for your business", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/27d116c68a0af696c2abce9f47d67131, and Dharmesh Mehta's post at https://sell.amazon.com/blog/announcements/fba-capacity, read 2026-11-10.

E2. **Usage and the two thresholds.** "capacity limits consider inventory that is on-hand… and shipments you've created that have not yet arrived". "overage fees will apply if your on-hand inventory…, not including open shipments, exceeds your capacity limit. Overage fees are calculated based on the highest estimated or confirmed limit that we provided for the given period." [official, same News post]. Staff, 2026-07-20: on-hand plus open shipments is the usage; exceeding the limit blocks new shipments; the overage fee applies only if on-hand alone exceeds; "Cancel unused open shipments — They count against your capacity even if you never send them" [official, staff post, Kris_Amazon] — https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/dc772f15-c8cf-4593-ad6c-7c51d67676db, read 2026-11-10. Removal orders are reflected in usage within 24 hours (Kris_Amazon, 2026-07-24, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/f3312979-4f82-448a-a192-84c1ea1e176e).

E3. **When limits are announced: two Amazon texts disagree.** The 2023 News post says "in the third full week of each month" and "the following two months". The 2026 staff post (Kris_Amazon, 2026-07-24) and the capacity help page v22 (read 2026-10-14 by the listing seat, not re-read) say the fourth full week and the next three months. ### F. Inbound placement service fee and inbound defect fee (as positioning cost)

F1. **Start and timing.** The inbound placement service fee applies from 2024-03-01, averaging $0.27 per unit for standard-size and $1.58 for Large Bulky, and is "charged 45 days after products are received" [official, 2024 letter].

F2. **The options and what each costs.** Amazon-optimized splits (four to five or more fulfilment centres): no fee. Partial splits (three to four): a reduced fee. Minimal splits (one to two): the highest fee. The fee shown at shipment creation is an estimate; the actual fee is charged 45 days after receipt on actual locations and quantities, and a shipment that deviates from its plan is recalculated. [official, staff post, Kris_Amazon 2026-10-23] — https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/3986c0e7-0399-4c87-9a3e-3a8fd083fe45, read 2026-11-10. The seller reads the charged amounts in the "FBA Inbound Placement Service Fee Report" in Seller Central; no SP-API report type exists for it (FBA Reports page; money playbook).

F3. **Dated changes.** 2025-01-15: Large Bulky placement fee lowered by an average $0.58 per unit "for minimal shipment splits"; fee waived for New Selection parent ASINs (shipments 2024-12-01 to 2025-03-31, first 100 units) [official, 2025 letter]. 2026-01-15: +$0.05 for standard-size minimal splits; one inbound defect fee averaging $0.60 per unit replaces the earlier defect fees [official, via sellersasksellers screenshot of G201411300; listing seat's read agrees]. 2027-01-15: the Central region merges into East (three inbound regions become two); the rate card is unchanged [official, Amazon Global Selling China notice, zh, helper's translation] — https://globalselling.amazon.com/news/news-notics-261105, read 2026-11-09.

F4. **Why it sits in a holding-cost file.** The placement fee is paid once per unit received; a seller who avoids it by sending to more locations pays in freight and time instead; a seller who avoids the low-inventory-level fee by sending more often pays it more often. The workflow is `areas/inbound.md`'s.

G2. **Liquidation.** A liquidation recovers "typically 5%–10% of the product's value" [official, staff post, Kris_Amazon 2026-10-06]. G4. **Fulfilment fee and its surcharges.** A 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge on FBA fulfilment fees has applied in the US and Canada since 2026-04-17; "The FBA fuel and logistics surcharge remains unchanged… since we introduced the surcharge in April 2026" [official, 2027 letter]. ## What practitioners add

Everything here is the named party's and is recorded as theirs. A number in this section is that party's number; none is Amazon's and none is cx's. URLs and dates are in *Pages read*.

P1. **Low-inventory-level fee: how it is actually triggered.** [opinion: SellerHow, 2026-09-02] the charge needs both windows under 28 days; the rate tier is set by the larger of the two figures; the grain is the FNSKU; inbound units do not count; Multi-Channel Fulfillment shipments count as demand; the "fewer than 20 units in 7 days" exemption is paid for in delivery speed; its own working figure is 40 or more days. [opinion: Amz520, undated, 2024 rules] describes the older parent-level grain, a weighted formula of its own, and observes that no fee is charged while a product is out of stock because no unit ships. [opinion: Red Stag Fulfillment, modified 2026-08-11] still describes the parent-level grain, which Amazon changed on 2026-01-15. [opinion: sellerboard, 2026-08-05] AWD stock does not count toward the FBA historical days of supply, and it counts the fee as part of the cost of a stock-out. ## Published research

Kept short; the survey (`beyond/sellers-tools-and-research.md`) owns the field.

- Berling (2008), "Holding cost determination: An activity-based cost approach", *International Journal of Production Economics* 112(2): 829–840 (abstract read): the holding cost rate used in inventory models is usually a rough percentage; an activity-based derivation gives a different, item-specific figure. [research]
- Azzi, Battini, Faccio, Persona and Sgarbossa (2014), "Inventory holding costs measurement: a multi-case study", *International Journal of Logistics Management* 25(1) (first pages read): measured holding costs vary widely between firms and between manual and automated warehouses, and firms rarely measure them. [research]
- Serrano (2017), "On the Cost of Capital in Inventory Models: The Case of Deterministic Demand", *Foundations and Trends in Technology, Information and Operations Management* (abstract read): the capital component of holding cost depends on how inventory is financed, not on one fixed rate. [research]
- The textbook form h = i·c, with i commonly quoted between 15% and 33% a year (Taylor & Francis knowledge page excerpting Huang; read as excerpts). [research]
- CSCMP's State of Logistics 2025 as reported by Supply Chain Digest (2025-06-13): the US inventory carrying cost aggregate. [research, via SCDigest]
- Cited by others and **not opened**: La Londe and Lambert (1977); Lambert and Mentzer (1979, via ReliaMag); Richardson (1995); Timme and Williams-Timme (2003); Chopra and Meindl; Nahmias; Silver, Pyke and Peterson; Zipkin. They are not relied on here.

What the research adds to this file: Amazon's charges are only the storage-and-handling part of carrying cost. Capital, obsolescence, shrinkage and insurance are not in any Amazon report, and the published ranges for the whole are far larger than Amazon's monthly storage rate alone. [seat]

## Stable enough for a skill

These have held across years of Amazon's text, or are structural. Rates are excluded on purpose.

- The monthly storage fee is charged on daily average volume per calendar month, by size class and season (January–September, October–December), on Amazon's measurement, in the month after. (A1, A2, A5; all marketplaces read)
- The low-inventory-level fee is a per-unit surcharge on the fulfilment fee of units shipped, not a charge on stock held; a product with no shipments pays none. (D2)
- Capacity usage is on-hand plus open shipments; the overage fee is on on-hand only. An open shipment never sent still counts toward usage. (E2)
- The inbound placement fee is charged 45 days after receipt on actual locations and quantities; the figure at shipment creation is an estimate. (F1, F2)
- Inventory age is a network-wide first-in-first-out accounting age. (C5)
- The charges are not the same set in every marketplace, the units differ (cubic feet, cubic metres, cubic decimetres, 1,000 cm³), and Japan's low-inventory threshold is 14 days where the US and Pan-EU use 28. (J)
- The US and Canadian storage charges change shape on 2027-02-01; anything describing the utilization surcharge or the aged surcharge on the 15th is dated to 2027-01-31 at the latest in those two stores, and Amazon had announced no such change for Europe, Japan or Mexico by 2026-11-10. (I, J)
- Amazon changes these fees on a yearly cycle: the US and Canada announce in October–December for mid-January and later dates; Europe in December for 1 February; Japan in December for April. (K)
- Amazon's charges are a part of carrying cost, not the whole. (Published research)

## What cx must read at run time

Each read gives a live value that this file must not freeze. Roles are Amazon's (section L). The door's report lifecycle and tokens are the door's and are not repeated.

R1. **Monthly storage and utilization surcharge, as estimated per FNSKU per fulfilment centre.** Report `GET_FBA_STORAGE_FEE_CHARGES_DATA`. **Role: Pricing or Amazon Fulfillment.** Can be requested or scheduled. Columns that matter: `month_of_charge`, `country_code`, `fulfillment_center`, `product_size_tier`, `item_volume` with `volume_units`, `average_quantity_on_hand`, `average_quantity_pending_removal`, `estimated_total_item_volume`, `storage_rate`, `base_rate`, `utilization_surcharge_rate`, `storage_utilization_ratio` with its units, `avg_qty_for_sus`, `est_vol_for_sus`, `est_base_msf`, `est_sus`, `estimated_monthly_storage_fee`, `dangerous_goods_storage_type`, the incentive columns, `currency`. Freshness: one month per request, available after the month closes. Pitfalls: Amazon calls the figures "estimated"; rows are per fulfilment centre and must be summed per FNSKU; the ratio is a seller-level number repeated on rows; the volume unit differs by country; after 2027-02-01 the surcharge columns have nothing to carry in the US and Canada, and whether age-tier columns are added is not announced.

R2. **Aged inventory surcharge, as charged.** Report `GET_FBA_FULFILLMENT_LONGTERM_STORAGE_FEE_CHARGES_DATA`. **Role: Pricing or Amazon Fulfillment.** Requested only; the date range "should be equal to one month". R3. **Age, the next assessment's estimate, days of supply and the low-inventory flags.** Report `GET_FBA_INVENTORY_PLANNING_DATA`. **Role: Amazon Fulfillment only.** Requested only. Columns: the `inv-age-*` family; each `quantity-to-be-charged-ais-*` / `estimated-ais-*` pair; `estimated-storage-cost-next-month`; `Inventory age snapshot date`; `storage-type`, `storage-volume`, `item-volume`; `sell-through`, `units-shipped-t7/t30/t60/t90`, `days-of-supply`, `weeks-of-cover-t30/t90`, `estimated-excess-quantity`; `historical-days-of-supply`, `Short term historical days of supply`, `Long term historical days of supply`, `Last updated date for Historical Days of Supply`, `fba-minimum-inventory-level`, `fba-inventory-level-health-status`, `Exempted from Low-Inventory-Level fee?`, `Low-Inventory-Level fee applied in current week?`; `Total Days of Supply (including units from open shipments)`; `recommended-action`, `recommended-removal-quantity`, `estimated-cost-savings-of-recommended-actions`. ## The seller's choices

Each is the seller's. What each changes is stated; none is picked.

- **How much cover to hold at FBA.** More cover lowers the chance of a stock-out and of the low-inventory-level fee, and raises monthly storage, (until 2027-01-31) the utilization ratio, the age of the tail, capacity usage and capital tied up. Less cover does the reverse. From 2027-02-01 in the US and Canada the utilization penalty goes and the age penalty steepens.
- **How often to send, and in what split.** Smaller, more frequent shipments keep age and storage down and pay freight and (for minimal splits) the placement fee more often; Amazon-optimized splits pay no placement fee and cost more in freight and receiving time across more locations.
- **Whether to hold the reserve at AWD, at a third-party warehouse, or at FBA.** AWD stock is outside FBA capacity, exempts a 70%-auto-replenished FNSKU from the low-inventory fee, and costs processing and transportation per box and per cubic foot on the way through; from 2027-03-01 AWD storage is age-based too. A third-party warehouse has none of Amazon's exemptions.
- **What to do with ageing stock.** Sell through at a lower price or with a deal (margin now), remove (removal fee, freight, the stock comes home), dispose (fee, stock lost), liquidate (a small recovery, fee), or keep paying. Until 2027-01-31 in the US the timing of a removal order against 11:59 PM Pacific on the 14th decides whether that month's surcharge is paid.
- **Whether to bid for capacity.** A reservation fee buys space; performance credits can offset it up to 100% if the space sells; an unsold reservation is a cost.
- **Whether to accept the low-inventory fee.** For a SKU that cannot be kept above the boundary (seasonal, supply-constrained, capacity-constrained) the fee is a per-unit cost of staying in FBA; the alternatives are a different fulfilment channel or more stock.
- **Which carrying-cost rate to use for the part Amazon does not bill** (capital, obsolescence). It is the seller's number; published ranges are in *Published research*.

## Positions

Each is a fact or a discipline. Marketplace scope is stated.

P-holding-costs-1. Monthly storage is charged on the daily average volume a seller's units occupy in a calendar month, by size class and by season (January–September, October–December), using Amazon's own measurement of the packaged unit. All marketplaces read. Sources: EU rate card 2 Nov 2026 p.21 and FAQ Q2; sell.amazon.ca, .com.au, .co.jp, vender.amazon.com.mx pricing pages. (A1, A2, J)

P-holding-costs-2. The storage fee for a month is charged in the following month, and the storage fee report gives it as an estimate per FNSKU per fulfilment centre with the base rate and the utilization surcharge in separate columns. US and every store the report serves. Sources: FBA Reports page (`GET_FBA_STORAGE_FEE_CHARGES_DATA`); seller-pasted Amazon e-mail in the EU forum; [snippet] of the US help page for the 7th–15th window. (A3, A5)

P-holding-costs-3. Stranded stock keeps paying storage; units under a submitted removal or liquidation order stop accruing storage and the aged surcharge. US; staff posts, not a policy page. Sources: Kris_Amazon 2026-10-06; Angie_Amazon 2026-02-09. (A6)

P-holding-costs-4. The storage utilization ratio is average daily volume stored divided by average daily volume shipped over the past 13 weeks, in weeks, computed per seller per size class on the last day of the month; it is not a per-SKU number. Europe in Amazon's words; the US report carries the same ratio. Sources: EU rate card p.21 and footnote 3; FBA Reports page. (B2)

P-holding-costs-5. The US storage utilization surcharge began 2023-04-01 and ends 2027-01-31; Canada's began 2023-09-01 and ends the same day; Europe's began 2023-05-01 and Japan's 2024-06-01, with no end announced by 2026-11-10. Sources: 2023 US News post; 2027 US letter; Canadian News posts; seller-pasted Amazon e-mail (EU); Japanese News post 2023-12-26. (B1, B5, J)

P-holding-costs-6. The utilization threshold is not one number across stores or years: 26 weeks in Europe in 2023, 22 weeks in Europe's 2026 card, 26 weeks in Japan's 2025 text. Sources: EU rate card; seller-pasted e-mail; Japanese News post 2025-03-28. (B3, J2, J6)

P-holding-costs-7. In Europe the utilization surcharge does not apply to sellers whose first shipment was under 52 weeks ago, to Individual accounts, or below 25 cubic feet of daily volume, and clothing, eyewear, shoes and bags are outside the ratio; in Japan the surcharge's scope includes apparel and shoes/bags at every size. Sources: EU rate card footnote 2; Japanese News post 2023-12-26. (J2, J6)

P-holding-costs-8. The US aged inventory surcharge is assessed on the 15th of each month and charged between the 18th and the 22nd, on top of the monthly storage fee, until the fee is merged on 2027-02-01. US. Sources: Angie_Amazon staff post 2026-02-09; Indy_Amazon 2026-09-19; 2027 letter. (C2, C8)

P-holding-costs-9. From 366 days the US aged surcharge is the greater of a per-cubic-foot charge and a per-unit charge; below 366 days it is per cubic foot only; the 181–270-day bands exclude clothing, shoes, bags, jewelry and watches. US, 2026. Sources: Angie_Amazon staff post 2026-02-09; 2026 summary page via sellersasksellers. (C3, C4)

P-holding-costs-10. Inventory age is counted first-in-first-out across the whole fulfilment network: a sale or removal is deducted from the oldest units, whatever unit is physically picked. US. Sources: Kris_Amazon 2026-10-06; Jameson_Amazon (thread of 2024-04-25). (C5)

P-holding-costs-11. A removal or disposal order submitted by 11:59 PM Pacific on the 14th takes those units out of that month's aged surcharge even if they have not physically left. US, until the surcharge ends. Sources: Angie_Amazon 2026-02-09; Indy_Amazon 2026-09-19. (C6)

P-holding-costs-12. The aged-surcharge start differs by store: 181 days in the US, Canada and Mexico, 241 days in the UK and the euro stores, 271 days in Japan; UAE and Saudi Arabia state 365 days. Sources: staff post 2026-02-09; sell.amazon.ca; vender.amazon.com.mx; EU rate card; Japanese News posts; sell.amazon.ae and .sa. (C3, J)

P-holding-costs-13. The planning report's aged-surcharge estimate columns differ by store: 366–455 and 456+ columns exist only for the US, Germany, the UK, France, Italy and Spain; other stores get a 365-plus column; Brazil gets the old long-term-storage columns; the report is not available in the Netherlands, Poland, Sweden or Belgium. Source: FBA Reports page. (C10, J9)

P-holding-costs-14. The low-inventory-level fee is a per-unit surcharge on the fulfilment fee of units shipped; it is not charged on stock held, and a product that ships nothing pays none. US, Canada, Pan-EU, Japan. Sources: Data Kiosk schema (`LOW_INVENTORY_LEVEL_FEE`); EU rate card p.10; 2024 Canadian News post. (D2)

P-holding-costs-15. Through 2027-01-31 the US fee applies only when both the 90-day and the 30-day historical days of supply are below 28 days, at FNSKU grain since 2026-01-15 (parent grain before). US. Sources: 2027 letter ("Today, the fee is assessed using trailing 30- and 90-day sales"); help page GV43F6S76Y9DHYRH v29 read 2026-10-14 by the listing seat; 2026 summary via sellersasksellers. (D3)

P-holding-costs-16. The fee's inventory count includes sellable units, transfers and units in processing and excludes inbound units and units pending removal; Multi-Channel and Remote Fulfillment shipments count as demand. So a shipment on its way does not lift the metric. US, 2026. Source: help page GV43F6S76Y9DHYRH v29 read 2026-10-14 by the listing seat, not re-read. (D3)

P-holding-costs-17. From 2027-02-01 the US fee is decided twice from one weekly inventory record: days of supply against the 28 days of shipments before the assessment and against the 28 days after; it is charged only if both are below 28, and therefore about four weeks in arrears. US (Canada announced the same sentence). Sources: 2027 letter; Amazon Global Selling China notice. (I1, I3)

P-holding-costs-18. The low-inventory threshold is 28 days in the US and Pan-EU and 14 days in Japan; the UK has no such fee in the 2026 rate card, and the Pan-EU fee does not apply in the Netherlands, Poland, Sweden, Belgium or Ireland. Sources: EU rate card p.10; Japanese News post 2024-04-18. (J2, J6)

P-holding-costs-19. Amazon's own pages disagree on the New Selection exemption window for the low-inventory fee: 180 days (help page v29, read 2026-10-14) and the first 200 units for 120 days (News post 2026-10-28). Both are recorded with their dates. US. (D4, B4)

P-holding-costs-20. Since 2024-05-15 a US product that sold fewer than 20 units in the past 7 days is outside the low-inventory fee; Canada's copy of the rule says 10 units. Sources: US News post 2024-05-02; Canadian copy. (D5)

P-holding-costs-21. Since 2023-03-01 (US, Europe), 2023-05-01 (Canada) and 2024-10-01 (Japan) FBA capacity is one monthly limit per storage type, measured in volume, with estimates for later months shown in the Capacity Monitor. Sources: 2023 US and EU News posts; Canadian 2023 post; Japanese post 2024-08-21. (E1, J)

P-holding-costs-22. Capacity usage is on-hand stock plus open shipments; exceeding the limit on that sum blocks new shipments; the overage fee is charged only when on-hand stock alone exceeds the limit, against the highest estimated or confirmed limit given for the period. US, Europe, Canada. Sources: 2023 News posts; Kris_Amazon 2026-07-20. (E2)

P-holding-costs-23. An open shipment that is never sent still counts toward capacity usage until it is cancelled. US. Source: Kris_Amazon 2026-07-20. (E2)

P-holding-costs-24. A Capacity Manager request is a bid: a reservation fee per cubic foot (per cubic metre in Japan), granted highest first, offset up to 100% by performance credits earned on sales from the extra space. US, Europe, Japan. Sources: 2023 News posts; Japanese post 2024-08-21; `CapacityReservationBillingEvent` in the Finances v0 model. (E5)

P-holding-costs-25. Japan abolished its storage overage fee on 2024-10-01; the US, Europe and Canada kept theirs. Sources: Japanese News post 2024-08-21; 2023 News posts. (J6, E2)

P-holding-costs-26. Amazon published IPI thresholds of 450 (from 2021-01-01) and 400 (from 2022-01-01); since 2023-03-01 its text says the limit is "influenced by" the IPI score among other factors and states no threshold. US. Sources: News posts 2020-12-22 and 2021-12-21; EU News post 2023-01-17. (E7)

P-holding-costs-27. Amazon's US texts disagree on when capacity limits are announced: third full week with two further months (2023 post) and fourth full week with three months (2026 staff post and help page v22). Both are recorded with their dates. (E3)

P-holding-costs-28. The US inbound placement service fee has applied since 2024-03-01, is zero for Amazon-optimized splits and highest for minimal splits, and is charged 45 days after receipt on actual locations and quantities; the figure at shipment creation is an estimate. US. Sources: 2024 letter; Kris_Amazon 2026-10-23. (F1, F2)

P-holding-costs-29. The European rate card and the Japanese announcements read carry no inbound placement service fee and no inbound defect fee. Europe, Japan (Japan by non-mention). Sources: EU rate card 2 Nov 2026; Japanese News posts 2023–2026. (J2, J6)

P-holding-costs-30. US removal and disposal fees are charged per unit as each unit is removed or disposed of, at unchanged rates, for orders created from 2026-03-01; the UK made the same change from 2026-05-01. US, UK. Sources: News post (article 2026-01-29, revised post 2026-02-16); UK News post 2026-04-09. (G1)

P-holding-costs-31. Liquidation in Canada and Europe costs a 15% referral fee on gross recovery plus a per-item processing fee; Amazon staff put typical US recovery at 5%–10% of the product's value. Sources: EU rate card p.23; Canadian 2026 News post; Kris_Amazon 2026-10-06. (G2, J)

P-holding-costs-32. AWD stock is outside FBA capacity limits, and an FNSKU at least 70% auto-replenished from AWD over 90 days is exempt from the low-inventory-level fee. US. Sources: Kris_Amazon 2026-07-20; EU AWD launch post; help page GV43F6S76Y9DHYRH v29 read 2026-10-14 by the listing seat. (E9, H4, D4)

P-holding-costs-33. AWD's bill has three parts — storage per cubic foot per month, processing per box, transportation per cubic foot — and from 2027-03-01 its US storage rate varies by age, with stock over 12 months at 2.5 times the standard rate. US. Sources: News post 2024-12-04; Amazon Global Selling China notice. (H1, H5)

P-holding-costs-34. On 2027-02-01 the US and Canada remove the storage utilization surcharge and fold the aged inventory surcharge into one age-based monthly storage rate; Amazon's letter says this gives "flat or lower fees for inventory that turns in under six months, higher fees for aged inventory". US, Canada. Sources: 2027 US letter; Canadian News post. (I1, J4)

P-holding-costs-35. As of 2026-11-10 the 2027 storage rates and age bands are not in any Amazon text readable without sign-in; the only table is a single third party's. A rate from that table is that party's report, not Amazon's. US. Sources: 2027 letter; Global Selling China notice (no rates); [opinion: Seller Essentials]. (I4, I5)

P-holding-costs-36. As of 2026-11-10 Amazon had announced no 2027 change to storage charges for the UK and Europe, Japan or Mexico; the 2027 design is a US and Canadian fact. Sources: EU rate card 2 Nov 2026; European, Japanese and Mexican News listings. (J)

P-holding-costs-37. Storage volume units differ by store: cubic feet (US, UK, UAE, Saudi Arabia), cubic metres (euro stores, Sweden, Canada, Australia), cubic decimetres (Mexico), 1,000 cm³ (Japan); the reports carry a unit column beside every volume. Sources: pricing pages and rate card; FBA Reports page. (J, A3)

P-holding-costs-38. In Europe storage is billed at the rate and in the currency of the country where the unit physically sits, the utilization surcharge is computed once for Germany, France, Italy and Spain together and once for the UK, and the low-inventory fee exists only for Pan-EU inventory. Europe. Sources: EU rate card pp.10, 21, 23; seller-pasted Amazon e-mail. (J2)

P-holding-costs-39. No SP-API report type exists for the charged inbound placement fee, the low-inventory-level fee, capacity limits or reservation fees; the limit appears in the API only inside overage-fee rows. Sources: FBA Reports page; Role Mappings for Types; money playbook `fulfilment-charges.md` (not re-read in full). (D6, F2, R4)

P-holding-costs-40. Account-level holding charges (storage, aged, placement, overage, reservation) never appear in an order-scoped financial read, and Amazon's seller Finances models (v0 `FeeType`, 2024-06-19 `breakdownType`) are free strings that enumerate no names for them; the strings are learned from live data. One tool names `FBAStorageFee` and `FBALongTermStorageFee` (the latter carrying the aged surcharge) and finds no fee type of their own for the utilization surcharge or the low-inventory fee in European data (its reading). Sources: `financesV0.json`; `finances_2024-06-19.json`; [opinion: AMALYTIX, 2026-10-20]; money playbook `fulfilment-charges.md`. (R5)

P-holding-costs-41. The fee preview report and the Product Fees estimate cover per-sale fees only; neither contains storage, age, placement, removal or capacity charges. Sources: FBA Reports page (`GET_FBA_ESTIMATED_FBA_FEES_TXT_DATA`); Product Fees API page; money playbook. (R7)

P-holding-costs-42. Roles: the storage, long-term-storage, fee-preview and removal reports need Pricing or Amazon Fulfillment; the planning and overage reports need Amazon Fulfillment; settlement and `listTransactions` need Finance and Accounting; `listFinancialEvents` needs Finance and Accounting, or Amazon Fulfillment in the NA and FE regions only. All are within the door's five. Sources: Role Mappings for Types and for Operations; FBA Reports page. (L)

P-holding-costs-43. Amazon's pages disagree on the role for the Data Kiosk Economics dataset (Brand Analytics on the API page; Selling Partner Insights in Amazon's correction); the dataset's preview is the only API read that states the low-inventory fee and the placement fee as amounts per SKU. Sources: Data Kiosk API page; changelog correction; schema file. (Reads that need a role outside the five)

P-holding-costs-44. The rate Amazon applied is carried on the row that applied it (`storage_rate`, `base_rate`, `utilization_surcharge_rate`, `rate-surcharge`, `charge_rate`); no operation returns a rate card, and Amazon changes these rates on a yearly cycle that differs by region. Sources: FBA Reports page; the calendar. (R9, K)

P-holding-costs-45. Days-of-cover figures quoted by practitioners (35–55, 40+, alert at 45) are theirs; 28 is Amazon's dated boundary for one fee in some stores, 181 and 241 and 271 are Amazon's dated starts of one surcharge in some stores, and none is a rule for how much a seller should hold. Sources: Amazon's as in D, C, J; [opinion: My Amazon Guy; SellerHow]. (P2)

P-holding-costs-46. Amazon's storage-side charges are one part of carrying cost; capital, obsolescence and shrinkage appear in no Amazon report, and the rate for them is the seller's number. Sources: [research] Berling 2008; Azzi et al. 2014; Serrano 2017. (Published research)

P-holding-costs-47. Amazon's US texts disagree on stock past 270 days: the aged-surcharge schedule effective 2026-01-16 prices sellable stock in bands from 271 to 456+ days, while an Amazon moderator wrote in about July 2026 that sellable stock aged over 270 days is removed under Amazon's policy even when automated removal is disabled, with Amazon choosing the disposition. Both are recorded with their dates. US. Sources: Angie_Amazon 2026-02-09; Arish_Amazon (thread of 2026-06-26); `areas/stuck-stock.md` §C4. (C3, C6, M) (gap fill 2026-11-10)

P-holding-costs-48. Amazon's Mexican pricing page states the prolonged-storage surcharge twice and differently: a table starting at 181 days (rates "actualizadas el 15/05/2025") and a line starting at more than 271 days at MXN 2.07 per dm³; both are on the same page. Mexico. Source: vender.amazon.com.mx/precios, read 2026-11-10. (J5) (gap fill 2026-11-10)

P-holding-costs-49. Amazon's Global Selling notice on the 2027 US fees suggests keeping four weeks of inventory ("建议始终保持4周库存水平"); this is Amazon's suggestion beside the fee's 28-day boundary, not a term of the fee. US. Source: globalselling.amazon.com news-notics-261105. (I2) (gap fill 2026-11-10)

P-holding-costs-50. The US low-inventory-level fee's grain and scope by date: parent product and standard-size from 2024-04-01; FNSKU, and Small and Large Bulky added, with Grocery exempt, from 2026-01-15; all oversize tiers added from 2027-02-01 (that last reported by a third party from Amazon's 2027 page). The 2026 change is stated on Amazon's help and summary pages as read on 2026-10-14 by another seat; Amazon's 2026 News post and letter do not state it. US. Sources: 2024 letter; help page GV43F6S76Y9DHYRH v29 and summary G201411300 v47 via the listing seat; [opinion: ppc.land]. (D8) (gap fill 2026-11-10)

The seat's own (no source; enters no skill):

- If the single third-party 2027 table is right, each 2027 age band is priced at or above the top of the 2026 base-plus-surcharge it replaces, and the 30-day steps disappear; "flat or lower … under six months" then holds only for stock under 181 days.
- A fee computed on a daily average by age tier has no single cleanup date, so the 14th-of-the-month removal rule probably has no successor after the last assessment; timing a removal would then save days, not a month.
- `avg_qty_for_sus` being smaller than `average_quantity_on_hand` is most likely the 0–30-day exclusion showing in the data.
- The 2027 low-inventory method makes the fee unknowable at the moment of shipping: a seller cannot see in week N whether week N's units will be surcharged, and a restock that lands inside the following 28 days changes nothing about the inventory record already taken, only (through sales) the second denominator.
- The Economics preview enum values for the utilization and aged surcharges will describe nothing in the US and Canada after 2027-02-01; a schema change should be expected and watched in the release notes.
- The non-US picture suggests Amazon rolls a storage design out from the US to Canada first, then Europe and Japan a year or more later with different parameters; nothing Amazon has said commits it to that.
