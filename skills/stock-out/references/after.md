# What is and is not measured after a stock-out

## Research

- No published study found measures a marketplace seller's search position or Best Sellers Rank after restocking (O21).
- The nearest Amazon evidence is a correlation of 90-day unavailability with worse (higher-numbered) sales rank across about 290,000 grocery products, in Etumnu, Chenarides & Jaenicke, *Agribusiness* (online 2025; issue 42(4), 2026; abstract only) (O21).
- Raval 2023 gives a Buy Box model in which being in stock is worth a price cut (O21).
- Other measured stock-out costs come from a catalogue retailer, an online grocer and stores, and from Lightning Deal availability signals (Anderson, Fitzsimons & Simester 2006; Jing & Lewis 2011; Cui, Zhang & Bassamboo 2018) (O21).
- In a mail-order field study, conversion on an ordered item fell when it was out of stock, and customers whose whole order was out of stock bought less afterwards; the setting is a catalogue retailer, not Amazon (O22).
- The study's figures: research/areas/running-out.md.
- No published study was found, by searches of 2026-11-10, of Amazon's capacity or storage limits, low-inventory-level fee, inbound placement fee or storage fees and sellers' stocking behaviour (T5).
- The academic work on FBA studies adoption, reputation and competition, and the one study linking Amazon unavailability with sales rank reports correlations (T5).

## Practitioners who published measurements

- EcomCrew (Dave Bryant; five ASINs, outages of days to months, Best Sellers Rank read off charts) found rank "almost always" back within four weeks with "no significant penalty", while one item "never recovered its historical sales rank" (O23).
- A Sellics case (Franz Jordan; one product, top three keywords, with a Sponsored Products push at restock that made most of the first week's sales) found rankings back a week after restock (O23).
- DataWeave reports rank falls after one day and after three days out for one customer's top-ten products, and Profitero reports a number of days for UK vendor brands to recover search placement (O23).
- DataWeave's, Sellics' and Profitero's figures: research/areas/running-out.md.
- Single cases report that Best Sellers Rank and keyword position recover differently: one seller on Seller Forums EU (2020-06-25) regained sales rank with ads while absent from the first seven result pages (O26).
- An anonymous poster on 知无不言 (2023-03-16) reports a product that held its keyword position through a month out of stock and lost it in the days after restock as conversion ran lower (O26).

## Practitioners without data

- Most practitioner pages (My Amazon Guy, SellerSprite, Repricer.com, Onramp Funds, Seller Labs, 跨境大师兄Peter) state that rank falls within three to seven days out of stock and takes two to six weeks to recover, without showing data, and the figures differ page to page (O24).
- The "three-day grace period" in Chinese practitioner writing cites no Amazon page; the earliest copy found is 藏龙荟's (2019-06-11) (O25).
- A 2021 post (K哥聊出海) prints the sentence with "two weeks", spliced onto Moss的精神家园's 2018 text, which describes his own routine for a gap under two weeks, not a grace period (O25).
- The claim that closing an out-of-stock listing "freezes" its rank is asserted by SPS Commerce, OJ Digital, My Amazon Guy and several Chinese writers and denied by sellers on Amazon's forums; neither side shows a comparison (O29).
- Estimates of recovery cost against lost sales range from about one-seventh of the total (sellerboard's worked example) to two to five times the lost sales (Onramp Funds) (O31).
- Inventory Hero notes that "Amazon does not publish a recovery multiplier" (O31).
